# Antifoulant & Online Wash Program (RS-CGC-03)
*Synthetic internal standard — for assignment use only.*

## 1. Why fouling matters
Cracked gas contains dienes and styrenes that polymerise on impeller and diffuser surfaces,
forming deposits ("fouling"). Fouling narrows the flow path, lowers polytropic efficiency,
raises discharge temperature and specific energy, and erodes surge margin. Left unmanaged it
shortens run length and forces an unplanned shutdown.

## 2. Antifoulant dosing
- Baseline dose: 6 ppm (ethane / light feed)
- Heavy feed (diene > 150 ppm): 12 - 18 ppm
- Never exceed 22 ppm without process-engineering approval.

## 3. Online water/oil wash trigger criteria
Schedule an online wash when ANY of the following is met:
- Polytropic efficiency falls 4 percentage points below the post-wash clean baseline, OR
- Final-stage discharge temperature rises 10 degC above the clean baseline at comparable flow, OR
- Specific energy consumption rises 8% above the post-wash baseline at comparable flow, OR
- Anti-surge margin at rated flow falls below 10%.

A typical run length between online washes is 100 - 120 days depending on feed slate. An online
wash recovers roughly 75 - 90% of lost efficiency. A wash that recovers less than 60% indicates
hard deposits and should trigger evaluation of an offline (turnaround) cleaning.

## 4. Wash-oil injection
Wash-oil injection rate is normally 120 - 300 kg/h, increased with diene loading. Wash-oil is
not a substitute for antifoulant; both are used together.

## 5. Economic note
An online wash costs production rate for several hours but restores efficiency and defers a far
more expensive offline cleaning. The wash decision is a trade-off between the energy penalty of
running fouled and the production/opportunity cost of washing.
